### Arithmetic enclosure derivation and limitations (2026-10-29)

Reading scope: Hargreaves' [Interval Analysis in MATLAB](https://www.tuhh.de/ti3/rump/intlab/narep416.pdf), sections 2.2 and 2.5, was read for endpoint inclusion and outward rounding; no INTLAB implementation was copied. Python's [Decimal documentation](https://docs.python.org/3.11/library/decimal.html), exact float construction, explicit contexts, endpoint rounding, square roots and adjacent-value methods, supplies the arithmetic engine contract. An attempted arXiv HTML quadrature paper was inaccessible and contributes no method evidence.

For x in [a,b] and y in [c,d], addition is enclosed by [a+c,b+d], subtraction by [a-d,b-c], and multiplication by the minimum/maximum of endpoint products, rounded down/up. Division requires a denominator interval excluding zero. Square is specialized when zero lies inside the interval; square-root monotonicity permits bracketing endpoint roots. Decimal's rounded square-root result is expanded to adjacent context values, with exact zero preserved. Applying these inclusion operations to the existing independently derived heat/orbit formulas encloses their exact-real values on the represented inputs. No transcendental orbital integration or reference solver is introduced.

The drift of a scalar enclosure uses absolute difference from its initial enclosure. A vector uses component differences and a Euclidean norm enclosure; it never subtracts vector magnitudes. Maxima of endpoint bounds enclose the sampled maximum. First-sample drift is identically zero. The original approximate metric d remains visible; if the exact metric lies in [L,U], max(|L-d|,|U-d|) bounds its formula-evaluation discrepancy. Serialization rounds endpoints and radius outward and includes endpoint-conversion error. Fifty decimal digits are a fixed implementation precision, not a claimed fifty-digit answer or a user tolerance. Interval dependency may widen a bound; it never justifies narrowing one without proof.

Heat balance bounds the piecewise-linear sampled-power trapezoid. For P=t^2 on [0,1], two saved power samples yield 1/2 while the continuous integral is 1/3: the resulting residual can have a tiny arithmetic bound and still reflect quadrature error. A test explicitly preserves that distinction. Input-parameter/state uncertainty, mapping, spatial quadrature, time integration error and unsampled extrema remain outside this bound. All outputs stay inconclusive and no registry method was enabled. Full applicability, accuracy targets, complete uncertainty treatment and scientific release still remain.

## Planned heat check families

### Absolute-metric decision derivation (2026-10-28)

The internal Python evidence helper accepts a nonnegative measured diagnostic d, target t and an independently justified absolute error bound u, all in matching units. Assuming `|d_true-d| <= u`, the true diagnostic lies in `[max(0,d-u), d+u]`. A whole interval at/below t supports passed_within_tolerance; a whole interval strictly above t supports flagged; overlap supports inconclusive. Unknown t or u also yields inconclusive. The implementation rounds nonzero-uncertainty endpoints outward with nextafter; this extra conservatism can make an exact boundary inconclusive. It is not a statistical confidence interval or a proof that the supplied u is valid. Zero uncertainty is allowed only as an explicit input whose justification belongs to the caller. There is no automatic zero bound or default tolerance, and no model-generated bound is trusted merely because it is numeric.

The accompanying drift routine measures `max_t |q(t)-q(0)|` for scalars and `max_t norm(q(t)-q(0))` for 2/3-component vectors. It compares vector differences, not differences of vector magnitudes: a direction reversal must not disappear. At least two samples are required. There is no division by the initial value, so zero total momentum is supported. This routine does not supply its own floating-point error bound; conversion, arithmetic, sampled-data, quadrature and reference uncertainty need independently justified treatment by each check. Units, timestamps, physical eligibility, evidence IDs and targets must be validated outside this internal helper. No check registry entry is enabled by its existence.

These IDs identify planned methods, not completed implementations. All obey Product SCOPE-03.

- `H_ENERGY`: source-free insulated/periodic thermal energy. Integrate temperature with physically appropriate density, heat capacity, and volume/quadrature weights. Require declared node/cell layout, boundary convention, and thickness/per-unit-thickness in 2D. Test against hand-integrated states and analytically constant-energy solutions. Reject a naive unweighted sum on incompatible meshes/layouts.
- `H_BALANCE`: accumulated energy change versus integrated volumetric heating and outward boundary heat flux. Verify sign convention, dimensions, time integration, and observation completeness. Prescribed temperature boundaries alone do not establish actual flux. Missing flux/source information makes this check unresolved/inapplicable or its result inconclusive; do not assume zero exchange.
- `H_TIME_REF`: baseline and two temporal refinements over the same physical horizon and observation coordinates, with spatial grid/model fixed. Distinguish actual internal step control from saved-output sampling. Verify that an accuracy-control change is supported before running.

### Heat diagnostic derivation and current scope (2026-10-28)

`packages/science/siphon_science/heat.py` implements thermal energy-change and balance measurements, without enabling H_ENERGY/H_BALANCE or issuing verdicts. For constant material properties, start from `rho*c*dT/dt = div(k*grad(T)) + s`. Integrate over the fixed volume and apply the divergence theorem. Defining outward physical heat flux as `q_out = -k*grad(T) dot n` yields `dE/dt = integral_V(s) - integral_boundary(q_out)`. Thus `Delta E = rho*c*sum(w_i*(T_i(t)-T_i(0)))` approximates the energy change using validated physical volume/quadrature weights. The balance residual is `Delta E - integral_time(P_source-P_outward)`. In SI, rho*c has units J/(m^3*K), weights have m^3, temperature changes K, powers W and residuals J.

Reading scope: inspected NIST FiPy's [finite-volume discretization reference](https://pages.nist.gov/fipy/en/stable/numerical/discret.html), transient, diffusion and source term integrals, to check volume weighting and the surface-integral sign derivation. FiPy describes cell averages and explicit volume/surface integration. Its treatment does not validate arbitrary nodal weights or Siphon's runtime. Nodal quadrature, periodic duplicate handling, geometry and physical thickness still require a separately validated mapping. Accepting a weights array does not prove that it describes the uploaded grid.

The energy routine accepts `(sample,x)` or `(sample,x,y)` float64 Kelvin arrays, matching positive weights, and positive constant density/heat capacity. Physical cross-section/thickness must already be included; no unlabelled per-unit-thickness output is called joules. It subtracts initial temperatures before weighted summation to avoid subtracting two large independently summed energies. It processes one spatial slice at a time. Negative modeled Kelvin values remain visible observations; this function does not conceal simulation defects through a positivity filter. Unit conversion and interpretation are still external prerequisites.

The balance routine requires at least two strictly increasing finite times, exact matching scalar series and an initial energy change of zero. Both power series must be independently extracted spatial integrals; deriving them from the same energy changes would make the check circular. It uses explicitly labelled piecewise-linear trapezoidal time integration with compensated cumulative summation. This quadrature is exact for linear power on each interval, but no uncertainty estimate is claimed for general sampled power. In particular P=t^2 over [0,1] has true integral1/3 while two-sample trapezoidal integration gives1/2. A nonzero residual can therefore reflect sampling/quadrature uncertainty and is not automatically a scientific failure. Constant-property, supported-grid, boundary/source and adapter eligibility remain mandatory.

Independent fixtures cover unequal weights (an unweighted temperature sum would incorrectly cancel), conservative redistribution, temperature-offset invariance, a 2D thickness-weighted uniform rise, constant outward cooling with irregular sample times, an intentional flux-sign reversal, and an analytic linearly driven balance. Shape/material/time/cap validation, non-finite values, arithmetic overflow and independent read-only output snapshots are covered. All scientific entries remain disabled pending complete applicability, uncertainty/accuracy and integration gates.

### Orbital diagnostic derivation and current scope (2026-10-28)

`packages/science/siphon_science/orbital.py` implements measurements only, not released checks or an orbit integrator. Inputs must already be converted to SI in a declared inertial frame. Isolated arrays have shape `(sample, body, dimension)` with dimension 2 or 3, fixed strictly positive masses, no collisions and complete mutually interacting bodies. Fixed-central arrays have shape `(sample, dimension)`, relative to the fixed source, with positive explicitly supplied mu. Planar angular vectors are embedded in three dimensions. Zero-mass tracers within an interacting system need separate treatment; they are rejected by this isolated diagnostic rather than silently discarded.

For the isolated model, derive `U = -G sum(i<j) mi*mj/rij`, `K = sum mi*|vi|^2/2`, `E = K+U`, `P = sum mi*vi`, `L = sum ri cross (mi*vi)` and `R = sum mi*ri / sum mi`. Newtonian pair forces are equal/opposite, hence `dP/dt=0`. Pair torques cancel because force is parallel to separation, hence `dL/dt=0` about a fixed inertial origin. Differentiating the pair potential gives `dU/dt = G sum(i<j) mi*mj (ri-rj) dot (vi-vj)/rij^3`; the kinetic derivative is its negative. This cancellation requires the stated force model and constant masses. From the definitions, `dR/dt=P/M`; a future center-of-mass residual must subtract this expected linear motion, not require stationary coordinates.

For the fixed central profile, `epsilon=|v|^2/2-mu/|r|` and `h=r cross v`. Substitution of `a=-mu*r/|r|^3` gives `d epsilon/dt=0` and `dh/dt=0`. These are specific quantities (m^2/s^2 and m^2/s), not total system joules or total angular momentum. There is no isolated-system total linear-momentum claim for this profile. Units for isolated outputs are J, kg*m^2/s, kg*m/s and m.

Independent fixtures: unit circular orbit yields epsilon=-1/2 and hz=1; a=2,e=1/2 apsides yield epsilon=-1/4 and hz=sqrt(3/2); equal unit masses at radius 1 with speed 1/2 yield E=-1/4,Lz=1,P=0. A 3-4-5 triangle with masses 1,2,3 and specified test velocities has K=6,U=-157/60,E=203/60,Lz=24,P=(-2,4,0),R=(1,2,0). Body permutations and proper rotations preserve the corresponding scalar/vector identities; a translation a obeys `L'=L+a cross P`. Test tolerances of order 1e-15 apply only to these unit-scale fixture arithmetic comparisons, not user accuracy criteria.

Implementation uses float64, compensated accumulation of pair potential with O(samples) extra storage instead of retaining all pair arrays, and accurate scalar summation across bodies. Overall arithmetic work is O(samples*bodies^2); caller-supplied sample/body caps must be server-owned. Positive finite parameters, exact shapes, finite inputs/results and zero separation are checked. Overflow/underflow produce an explicit unrepresentable-result error, never a scientific defect verdict. Compensated sums do not provide a certified rounding-error bound. Output arrays are read-only. Timestamp validation, SI conversion, frame/provenance validation, uncertainty bounds, drift metrics, appropriate targets, hosted time limits and check release remain separate unfinished work.

Reading scope: revisited Rein and Spiegel's [IAS15 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1409.4779v2), section 3.4 and section 4.1, for why energy behavior and phase accuracy are distinct. Inspected the official [REBOUND diagnostic source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hannorein/rebound/main/src/tools.c), energy/angular-momentum functions, to cross-check the standard pair-counting and vector definitions. No REBOUND algorithm/source was copied and no installed REBOUND runtime comparison is claimed. The mutable main-branch link is research context, not a pinned numerical dependency. The independently derived formulas and fixtures above supply the current local verification; multi-body trajectory integration and independent reference accuracy are not yet established.
